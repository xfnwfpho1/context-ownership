# Aurora Station — Logistics and Resupply

**2 convoys per season** — the opening convoy in October and the
mid-season convoy in December. A third convoy runs only when
failure-recovery demands it (generator loss, medical, or a runway
re-survey), and it costs the season its fuel margin, so it is a
station-leader decision made with the sponsor on the 16:00 window, in
that order, on the record.

## Per-convoy limits

| Limit | Value |
|---|---|
| Max sled load (gross, including fuel drums) | 800 kg |
| Fuel uplift per convoy | 6,000 L |
| Fresh-food floor on station | 14 days at all times |
| Waste retrograde | 100% — all waste leaves on the convoys |

The 6,000 L uplift covers the generator's planned 4 hours per day of
top-up (POWER.md owns the generator discipline) plus a 40% storm
margin for the deep season. If the forecast calls for more than four
blizzard-grounded weeks, the logistics coordinator pre-requests a
fuel top-up on the mid-season convoy rather than gambling on the
margin.

## What each convoy carries

- **Opening (October)**: crew rotation-in, opening-up spares, the
  season's science consumables (SCIENCE.md's instrument list), runway
  grooming gear.
- **Mid-season (December)**: crew rotation, fresh spares (comms LNB
  and modulator rotation — COMMS.md owns that spares manifest), restock
  to the fresh-food floor, retrograde of season-to-date waste.
- **Closing (March)**: crew rotation-out, full retrograde, data
  archives duplicated to the physical mail sack as belt-and-braces
  with the downlink (MISSION.md owns the closing-phase calendar).

## Runway and route

The runway is surveyed at opening and after every storm above 20 m/s.
Convoy staging uses the same route every time — flagged and GPS-pinned
in October, re-pinned by the mid-season crew. Sled trains travel only
in daylight windows with winds under 15 m/s; a convoy caught out
over-night digs in and calls the 00:00 pass (COMMS.md).
